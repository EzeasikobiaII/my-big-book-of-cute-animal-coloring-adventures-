## 2. Book Concept

A fun and engaging coloring adventure featuring 50 cute animals in simple, imaginative settings, followed by creative activities that encourage children to draw, observe, match, and explore the animal world.

The illustrations must be friendly, recognizable, and easy for children ages 4–8 to color.

The finished book should provide an enjoyable coloring experience while helping children explore different animals, their distinctive features, and their natural environments.

## 3. Illustration Style

Every coloring illustration must use:

- Bold, clean black outlines.
- Large, open coloring areas.
- Simple, recognizable shapes.
- Cute and friendly animal expressions.
- Spacious, uncluttered backgrounds.
- Clear separation between objects.
- White backgrounds with black line art only.
- No gray shading or gradients.
- No unnecessary tiny details.
- No copyrighted characters or direct copies of existing artwork.
- No brand logos or trademarks.
- No frightening or inappropriate imagery.
- No unnecessary solid-black areas that prevent children from coloring.

Artwork must be designed specifically for children ages 4–8.

### 3.1 Line-Art Requirements

1. Use clear black outlines.
2. Keep the background white.
3. Avoid grayscale fills, gradients, and heavy shading.
4. Avoid excessive hatching, stippling, and tiny decorative marks.
5. Maintain sufficient white space for crayons and colored pencils.
6. Ensure the artwork remains clear when printed.
7. Use consistent line weight throughout each page.
8. Keep all important artwork within the safe printing area.

### 3.2 Child-Friendly Composition

Each illustration should:

- Feature one primary animal.
- Make the animal large enough to color comfortably.
- Show a recognizable and appropriate animal pose.
- Use a friendly, approachable expression.
- Include only a reasonable number of background objects.
- Separate overlapping objects clearly.
- Avoid overly complicated scenes.

The animal must remain the main focus of the page.

### 3.3 Animal Research, Accuracy, and Illustration Method

**This research-first workflow is mandatory for all 50 animal coloring pages.**

The purpose is to ensure that every illustration is factually informed, recognizable, anatomically plausible, suitable for children ages 4–8, and consistent with the established Master Sheet.

#### Step 1: Identify the Exact Animal

Confirm the animal's common name and, when necessary, its species or type.

Pay particular attention to animals that may be confused with similar species.

#### Step 2: Research Before Generating

Research each animal **before** generating or correcting its illustration. Do not rely on memory alone for distinctive anatomy, proportions, features, or habitat.

Prioritize these sources:

1. Government wildlife agencies and natural-history museums.
2. Accredited zoos and aquariums.
3. Universities and peer-reviewed scientific or educational sources.
4. Established conservation organizations with species profiles.

Use at least two reliable sources when practical, especially for unusual animals or animals with easily confused identifying features.

Prefer sources that specifically discuss the animal being illustrated.

Do not rely solely on unsourced social posts, generic image captions, or AI-generated descriptions as factual authority.

#### Step 3: Record the Animal's Defining Features

Before generating, note:

- Body shape and proportions.
- Distinctive anatomy, such as ears, horns, tail, feet, flippers, beak, scales, or characteristic markings.
- Facial and muzzle shape, where relevant.
- A recognizable and plausible pose.
- Natural habitat and a few appropriate environmental details.
- Features that distinguish this animal from similar animals.

Record only facts that help the illustration. Do not overload the page with scientific detail.

#### Step 4: Create an Original, Child-Friendly Illustration Brief

Translate the verified facts into a concise prompt that describes the animal's defining characteristics and a suitable habitat while following the established illustration template.

The animal must be recognizable, friendly, anatomically plausible, and easy for children ages 4–8 to color.

Use reference sources to understand the animal, not to reproduce a specific photograph, illustration, or artist's distinctive style.

#### Step 5: Generate Using the Existing Master Sheet

Preserve the established composition, rounded border, exact header, animal-name area, and outlined-letter rules.

Use bold black outlines, white open interiors, and an uncluttered background.

Do not introduce new stylistic choices unless requested.

#### Step 6: Fact-Check the Generated Illustration

Compare the illustration with the research notes.

Verify that:

- The animal's defining features are correct.
- Body proportions and anatomy are plausible.
- The number and placement of limbs and other features are appropriate.
- The face, ears, tail, markings, and other distinctive characteristics match the intended animal.
- The pose is recognizable.
- The habitat is appropriate.
- No contradictory or confusing features have been introduced.

Correct material inaccuracies before approval.

A cute illustration is not automatically an accurate illustration.

#### Step 7: Complete Coloring-Page and Print Checks

Inspect the image for:

- Pure black line art on a white background.
- No unwanted color, gray shading, gradients, or grayscale fills.
- Bold, clean outlines with reasonably consistent line weight.
- Large, open coloring areas suitable for ages 4–8.
- No excessive fine detail or crowded background.
- Correct header wording and outlined lettering with white interiors.
- Correct animal name with outlined lettering and white interiors.
- Consistent template, border, spacing, and layout.
- Safe margins around all important content.
- Verified image dimensions and print resolution.

The target remains 2550 × 3300 pixels at 300 DPI for an 8.5 × 11-inch portrait coloring page.

Do not assume an image meets the print specifications without checking its actual dimensions and file properties.

#### Step 8: Record Sources and Approval Status

Keep source names and links, a brief note of verified identifying features, and the page's quality-control status in the project notes or page record.

Use one of these statuses:

- **Needs QC:** Research, factual accuracy, visual quality, or print requirements have not all been verified.
- **Approved:** Research has been completed, the illustration has been fact-checked, and all required visual and print checks have passed.

Do not mark an illustration as approved while a required check remains unverified.

**Non-negotiable principle:** Research happens before generation. Post-generation research verifies and corrects the illustration; it does not replace the initial research step.

## 4. Permanent Header and Lettering Rules

**These rules are mandatory for all 50 animal coloring pages.**

### 4.1 Standard Page Header

Every animal coloring page must use this exact heading:

**COLOR • IMAGINE • HAVE FUN!**

The header must follow these requirements:

- Every letter must have a bold black outline.
- Every letter must have a white, unshaded interior.
- Children must be able to color inside every letter.
- No header letter may be filled with solid black.
- No gray fills, gradients, or shading may appear inside the letters.
- Keep the header wording, placement, and general design consistent across pages.

### 4.2 Animal-Name Lettering

- Place the correct animal name in the established title area below the illustration.
- Use large, bold, outlined letters with white, unshaded interiors.
- Children must be able to color inside every letter.
- Never use solid-black animal-name lettering.
- Keep title size, placement, spacing, and style consistent across pages.

Paw-print decorations may remain solid black where the approved template uses them.

Do not change the established template or introduce unrequested stylistic variations.
